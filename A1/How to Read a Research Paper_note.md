# How to Read a Research Paper

> Based on **How to Read a Paper** by S. Keshav

## Main Idea

Do not read a research paper from beginning to end immediately. Read it in three passes. Each pass gives you a deeper level of understanding.

## Pass 1: Get the Big Picture

The goal is to understand what the paper is about and decide whether you should continue reading.

### What to read

- Title
- Abstract
- Introduction
- Section headings
- Conclusion
- References

### The Five Cs

After the first pass, answer these questions:

1. **Category:** What type of paper is it?
2. **Context:** What previous work is related to it?
3. **Correctness:** Do the assumptions seem reasonable?
4. **Contributions:** What are the main contributions?
5. **Clarity:** Is the paper clearly written?

After this pass, decide whether the paper is useful enough to continue.

## Pass 2: Understand the Content

Read the paper more carefully, but do not spend too much time on difficult proofs or small details.

### What to do

- Write down the main points.
- Add short comments or questions.
- Study the figures, diagrams, and tables.
- Check whether graph axes and labels are clear.
- Check whether the evidence supports the conclusions.
- Mark useful references for later reading.

After this pass, you should be able to explain the paper's main idea and supporting evidence to another person.

If the paper is still difficult, you can:

- Stop reading it.
- Read background material first and return later.
- Continue to the third pass.

## Pass 3: Understand It Deeply

The goal is to rebuild the work in your mind as if you were the author.

### What to do

- Recreate the author's reasoning and method.
- Identify and question every assumption.
- Compare the author's approach with your own ideas.
- Look for weaknesses, missing references, or technical problems.
- Write down possible improvements and future research ideas.

After this pass, you should understand the paper's structure, strengths, weaknesses, assumptions, and methods in detail.

## How to Do a Literature Survey

1. Search for several recent papers using good keywords.
2. Complete the first pass on each paper.
3. Read their related-work sections.
4. Identify repeated papers, authors, and research groups.
5. Find recent work from the important authors and conferences.
6. Read the most useful papers with the second pass.
7. Follow important citations and repeat the process when needed.

## Quick Checklist

- [ ] What problem does the paper solve?
- [ ] Why is the problem important?
- [ ] What method does the paper use?
- [ ] What are the main contributions?
- [ ] What evidence supports the results?
- [ ] Are the assumptions reasonable?
- [ ] What are the strengths and weaknesses?
- [ ] Which references should I read next?
- [ ] What new research ideas did I get?

## Final Reminder

> Read with a clear purpose. Start with the big picture, then increase the level of detail only when necessary.
